SiriMouse

Turn your old Apple TV Siri Remote into a presentation clicker, a mouse and a media remote for your Mac.

Three modes, one remote

Press the TV button to switch modes. A short message on screen shows which mode you're in, and the menu bar icon changes to match.

Presenter

Run Keynote, PowerPoint, Google Slides or a PDF from across the room.

  • Click or swipe right for the next slide
  • Swipe left, or click the left edge, to go back
  • Menu exits the slideshow

Mouse

The glass touch surface works like a small trackpad.

  • Slide your finger to move the pointer
  • Click to click; hold the click and slide to drag
  • Play/Pause right-clicks; slide on the right edge to scroll

Media

Control Music, Spotify, podcasts or a video from the couch.

  • Click to play or pause
  • Swipe left or right to change track
  • Swipe up or down to change the volume

Siri button

Choose what the Siri button does from the menu bar. Both options listen through your Mac's microphone, or whichever input you've picked in Sound settings, such as AirPods.

Open Siri

Press the Siri button to start Siri on your Mac, just like clicking its icon.

Dictate text

Hold the Siri button and speak, then let go. What you said is typed into the app you're using. Recognition runs on your Mac when it can.

Get started

Install the app, pair the remote, then allow access.

  1. Install

    Download the zip, unzip it, move SiriMouse to your Applications folder and open it. It appears in the menu bar, not the Dock.

  2. Pair the remote

    Unpair it from your Apple TV first. Then open System Settings → Bluetooth, hold Menu + Volume Up for about 5 seconds near your Mac, and click Connect.

  3. Allow access

    In System Settings → Privacy & Security, turn on SiriMouse under Input Monitoring (to read the remote) and Accessibility (to press keys and move the pointer).

Questions

Which remote does it work with?
The 1st-generation Siri Remote (2015), the thin one with the black glass touch surface.
Does it use the remote's microphone?
No. macOS doesn't let apps use the remote's microphone, so SiriMouse uses your Mac's microphone instead. If you present far from your laptop, choose AirPods or a wireless mic as the input in Sound settings.
Does it send any data anywhere?
No. SiriMouse doesn't connect to the internet. Everything runs on your Mac.
Can I start it automatically?
Yes. Turn on Launch at Login in the SiriMouse menu.
Something isn't working
Choose Open Log from the SiriMouse menu and open an issue with what you see. The touch surface relies on a part of macOS that Apple could change in a future update; if that happens, the buttons keep working.
